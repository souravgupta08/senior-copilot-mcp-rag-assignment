# Centrifugal Pump Maintenance Manual (MM-PMP-002)

## Likely Causes of High Discharge Pressure

The most likely causes of high discharge pressure on a centrifugal pump are:

- a downstream control valve that is closing or stuck (check valve positioner and instrument air),
- a blocked or partially closed discharge line or non-return valve,
- a faulty pressure transmitter giving a false high reading,
- operation at very low flow (pump running near shut-off head).

Maintenance should compare the pressure alarm times with valve position alarms. If the valve alarm comes first, the valve is the likely root cause.

## Bearing Inspection

High bearing temperature is usually caused by low or contaminated lube oil, bearing wear, or misalignment.
Take an oil sample and check for water and metal particles.
Replace bearings if vibration analysis shows bearing defect frequencies.

## Vibration Troubleshooting

Compare vibration with the baseline recorded after the last overhaul.
Common causes: misalignment, unbalance, loose foundation bolts, cavitation and bearing damage.
Check coupling alignment after any motor or pump replacement.

## Mechanical Seal

A seal leak alarm requires a visual check with proper PPE. Small leaks can be monitored; a growing leak requires a planned seal replacement.

## Restart After Trip

Before restarting a pump after a protection trip, the cause must be identified and recorded. The restart must be approved by the shift supervisor.
